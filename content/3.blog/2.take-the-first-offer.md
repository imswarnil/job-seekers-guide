The offer is ₹3.6 lakh. It is a service company. The work is maintenance on a
codebase somebody wrote in 2014. The internet has told you, repeatedly, that this
is beneath you.

Take it.

## What the offer actually buys

It is not the salary. At that number the salary is close to irrelevant. What you
are buying is a change of category: you stop being a candidate with no experience
and become an engineer with a job. Every conversation after that is different.

- Recruiters answer differently. "Currently at X, looking to move" outperforms "fresher, looking for opportunity" by a margin that has nothing to do with skill.
- Interviews get easier, because you have real work to talk about instead of college projects.
- You learn things that cannot be learned alone: code review, deployment, incident response, and how to disagree with a colleague professionally.

## The maths nobody does

Eleven months of unemployed preparation against eleven months of employed
experience. At the end of both, you interview for the same role. The employed
version of you has: salary earned, production experience, a reference, and
answers to behavioural questions. The unemployed version has more LeetCode.

One of those two is a better candidate, and it is not close.

## The two real objections

**"It will typecast me."** Two years of experience typecasts nobody. Switching
after eighteen months to two years is the single most common career move in this
industry and everyone interviewing you has done it themselves.

**"I will stop studying."** This one is legitimate, and it is a scheduling
problem, not a reason to decline. Five hours a week, protected, is enough to keep
the foundations moving while you work. It is also considerably easier to study
when you are not simultaneously afraid about money.

## When not to take it

If it requires a bond with a penalty you cannot pay, or an unpaid training
period longer than a month, or asks *you* to pay *them*. Those are not first
jobs, they are a different kind of business, and walking away from them is not
the same decision as the one this piece is about.
