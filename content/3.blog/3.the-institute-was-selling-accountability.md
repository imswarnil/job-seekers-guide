Everything a coaching institute teaches is available free. Not approximately —
exactly. The same syllabus, taught better, by people who wrote the language, on
YouTube, in documentation, in open courseware. This has been true for a decade.

People keep paying. They are not stupid. They are buying four things that are
genuinely hard to get for free, and if you want to replace the institute you have
to replace all four rather than just downloading the videos.

## One: sequence

Free content has no order. Two thousand tutorials, each assuming a different
starting point, and no way to tell which one is next. The institute's real
product is a numbered list. **Replace it with:** a path you commit to in advance
and do not re-plan weekly. Re-planning feels like progress and is not.

## Two: filtering

Free content does not tell you what to skip, because the person who made it
thinks their topic matters. The institute's syllabus is defined as much by
exclusions as inclusions. **Replace it with:** an explicit skip list, written
down, with the reason next to each item. Ours is [here](/docs/curriculum/what-we-exclude).

## Three: a deadline you did not set

The batch starts Monday whether or not you feel ready. This is the part that is
genuinely hard to reproduce alone, because a deadline you set for yourself can be
moved by you, silently, at 11pm, with no consequence. **Replace it with:**
another person. A study partner you report to weekly is closer to the institute's
actual mechanism than any app. Failing that, publish your progress somewhere
public enough to be embarrassing.

## Four: sunk cost

₹80,000 already spent is a powerful reason to attend on the evening you do not
feel like it. Free has no such lever, and pretending otherwise is dishonest.
**Replace it with:** a streak you do not want to break, a visible progress bar,
and finished artefacts you can look at. Weaker than eighty thousand rupees.
Cheaper by eighty thousand rupees.

## The honest accounting

Three of the four are fully replaceable and the fourth is partly. That is the
whole thesis of this platform: give away the sequence and the filtering, build
in as much of the accountability as software can carry, and be truthful about
the part that still has to come from you.
