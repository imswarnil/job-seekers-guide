Somebody on your feed built a full-stack application in a weekend. Somebody else
cleared four interview rounds at a company you have heard of. You have been
"learning to code" for five months and cannot confidently explain what a REST API
is.

Here is what you are not being shown.

## The timeline is compressed on purpose

The person who built the app in a weekend had built four like it before. The
weekend is the part that is interesting to post about; the two years that made
the weekend possible are not. Every visible achievement is the tip of an
invisible amount of repetition, and the platform showing it to you is optimised
to hide the repetition, because the repetition is boring.

## Five months is not a long time

A computer science degree is four years. A good bootcamp is six months of full
time work, which is roughly a thousand hours. If you are studying eight hours a
week around a job, five months is about a hundred and seventy hours. You are not
slow. You are seventeen percent of the way through the first pass, on schedule,
and comparing yourself to people at the thousand-hour mark.

## The specific fix

Stop measuring in months and start measuring in finished things. A lesson
completed, a program that runs, a project deployed somewhere with a URL. Months
pass whether or not you study; finished things do not.

Then, once a week, look at the list. It will be longer than you think, because
the anxious version of you does the counting from memory and memory only keeps
the failures.

## What actually predicts an offer

Not how fast you learned. In order: whether you finished a real project, whether
you can explain your own work clearly, whether you kept applying after the
rejections, and whether you understood the foundations well enough to answer the
question one level below the one you were asked.

None of those four is a speed contest. All of them reward the person who was
still there in month nine.
