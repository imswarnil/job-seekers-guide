Saying this plainly unblocks a large number of capable people who ruled
themselves out of software for reasons that were never true.

| Excluded | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Mathematics** — calculus, discrete maths, engineering maths | Not required for a software job. This is the single most common reason people wrongly rule themselves out. |
| Aptitude and reasoning drills | Some companies test it. It is a short, separate prep — not a subject. |
| Microprocessors, digital electronics | Real computer science subjects. Not load-bearing for software employment. |
| Certification farming | Projects prove more, at a fraction of the cost. |
| "Learn 12 frameworks" | One language, one track, done properly. |
| Competitive programming as a lifestyle | Useful for a specific kind of interview. A poor use of your first six months. |

## On maths specifically

You need arithmetic, a feel for orders of magnitude, and enough logic to reason
about conditions and loops. That is it. You do not need calculus. You do not need
discrete mathematics. You will meet Big-O notation, and we teach the two hours of
it that matter rather than the semester of it that does not.

If a curriculum tells you otherwise, ask it which line of production code it is
preparing you to write.

## On skipping

Every course here marks the lessons that are safe to skip on a first pass, and
says what you lose by skipping them. Skipping deliberately is studying. Skipping
because you got lost is drifting, and the difference is whether somebody told you
which one you were doing.
