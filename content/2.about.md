audience:
  title: Who it is for
  body: |
    People with potential who are lost. Mechanical, civil, B.Sc., commerce and
    completely non-technical graduates. Computer science graduates who were never
    told what the roles actually are. Anybody who has a family conversation waiting
    at home about a safer plan, and no information to argue against it with.

principles:
  - title: Direction is the product, not content
    body: We curate, sequence and filter — and we say out loud what to skip.
  - title: Foundations before tools
    body: Tools change every 18 months; OS, DBMS, networks, OOP and DSA do not.
  - title: Orientation before choice
    body: Everyone starts at the same place, so the choice that follows is informed rather than random.
  - title: One language, learned properly
    body: We recommend Java and defend it. What we refuse to endorse is learning four languages badly.
  - title: This is the IIT curriculum, taught honestly
    body: The subjects are not secret. We cannot give you the campus; we can give you the syllabus and an honest explanation of each topic.
  - title: Job search is a syllabus, not an afterthought
    body: It converts everything before it into income.
  - title: Honest about money and outcomes
    body: No "100% placement" claims. No guaranteed packages.
  - title: The path is data, not code
    body: Serving a new kind of learner means composing a sequence, not shipping a release.
  - title: Free core, forever
    body: Anything monetised later must be genuinely additional — mentorship, review, cohorts — never the map itself.
  - title: Accountability is a feature
    body: Progress, streaks, resume-where-you-left-off, a player that knows your next lesson. This is what the institute really sold.

excluded:
  - what: Mathematics — calculus, discrete maths, engineering maths
    why: Not required for a software job. The single most common reason capable people rule themselves out.
  - what: Aptitude and reasoning drills
    why: Some companies test it. It is a short, separate prep — not a subject.
  - what: Microprocessors, digital electronics
    why: Real computer science subjects. Not load-bearing for software employment.
  - what: Certification farming
    why: Projects prove more, at a fraction of the cost.
  - what: "\"Learn 12 frameworks\""
    why: One language, one track, done properly.
  - what: Competitive programming as a lifestyle
    why: Useful for one kind of interview. A poor use of your first six months.

nonGoals:
  - A job board. We teach the hunt; we do not broker the roles.
  - A placement agency. No fees, no guarantees, no bonds.
  - An interview-question dump. Patterns and understanding, not 500 MCQs.
  - A maths-heavy CS degree.
  - A chase after every trending tool.

built:
  title: How it is built
  body: |
    Everything on this site is markdown in a public repository. There is no
    database, no account, and no tracking — your progress is stored in your own
    browser and never leaves your device.

    That is not a technical footnote. It is the reason a wrong lesson can be
    corrected by anybody who spots it, and the reason nothing here can quietly
    stop being free.
