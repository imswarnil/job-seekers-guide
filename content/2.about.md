## The mission

::callout{icon="i-lucide-compass" color="primary"}
Getting a job in software is not a talent problem. It is a **navigation
problem**. Content is abundant and free; direction is scarce and expensive.
We invert that.
::

The lectures exist. The documentation exists. The tutorials exist. What a
₹80,000 institute sells is **sequence, filtering, and somebody telling you what
to do next** — plus the accountability of having paid. The first three of those
cost nothing to give away.

So they are given away. One ordered path, from "what is software" to your first
offer, free and staying free.

## Who it is for

People with potential who are lost. Mechanical, civil, B.Sc., commerce and
completely non-technical graduates. Computer science graduates who were never
told what the roles actually are. Anybody who has a family conversation waiting
at home about a safer plan, and no information to argue against it with.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-user-round" color="secondary" to="/series"}
**Including me.** I was an average student who could not clear a single written
round in college, and I am now a Salesforce engineer in Europe. The whole route
— every rejection, every salary figure — is in my story.
::

## The ten principles

Every lesson, every page and every design decision here is measured against
these.

1. **Direction is the product, not content.** We curate, sequence and filter — and we say out loud what to skip.
2. **Foundations before tools.** Tools change every 18 months; OS, DBMS, networks, OOP and DSA do not.
3. **Orientation before choice.** Everyone starts at the same place, so the choice that follows is informed rather than random.
4. **One language, learned properly.** We recommend Java and defend it. What we refuse to endorse is learning four languages badly.
5. **This is the IIT curriculum, taught honestly.** The subjects are not secret. We cannot give you the campus; we can give you the syllabus and an honest explanation of each topic.
6. **Job search is a syllabus, not an afterthought.** It converts everything before it into income.
7. **Honest about money and outcomes.** No "100% placement" claims. No guaranteed packages.
8. **The path is data, not code.** Serving a new kind of learner means composing a sequence, not shipping a release.
9. **Free core, forever.** Anything monetised later must be genuinely additional — mentorship, review, cohorts — never the map itself.
10. **Accountability is a feature.** Progress, streaks, resume-where-you-left-off, a player that knows your next lesson. This is what the institute really sold.

## What is deliberately left out

A lesson that says "skip this, here is why" is as valuable as one that teaches.

| Excluded | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Mathematics** — calculus, discrete maths, engineering maths | Not required for a software job. The single most common reason capable people rule themselves out. |
| Aptitude and reasoning drills | Some companies test it. It is a short, separate prep — not a subject. |
| Microprocessors, digital electronics | Real computer science subjects. Not load-bearing for software employment. |
| Certification farming | Projects prove more, at a fraction of the cost. |
| "Learn 12 frameworks" | One language, one track, done properly. |
| Competitive programming as a lifestyle | Useful for one kind of interview. A poor use of your first six months. |

## Explicit non-goals

- **Not** a job board. We teach the hunt; we do not broker the roles.
- **Not** a placement agency. No fees, no guarantees, no bonds.
- **Not** an interview-question dump. Patterns and understanding, not 500 MCQs.
- **Not** a maths-heavy CS degree.
- **Not** chasing every trending tool.

## How it is built

Everything on this site is markdown in a public repository. There is no
database, no account, and no tracking — your progress is stored in your own
browser and never leaves your device.

That is not a technical footnote. It is the reason a wrong lesson can be
corrected by anybody who spots it, and the reason nothing here can quietly stop
being free.
