**Job Seekers Guide** is one path, walked end to end. Not a catalogue of courses
you browse and not a pile of documentation you search — a sequence, from "what is
software" to your first offer, with the next step always marked.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-compass" color="primary"}
Getting a job in software is not a talent problem. It is a **navigation problem**.
Content is abundant and free; direction is scarce and expensive. We invert that.
::

The lectures exist. The docs exist. The blog series exist. What a ₹80,000 institute
sells is sequence, filtering, and somebody telling you what to do next — plus the
accountability of having paid. The first three of those cost nothing to give away.

## What the path covers

1. **Orientation** — what the IT industry actually is, before you pick anything.
2. **Foundation** — the computer science subjects that matter in real work.
3. **One language, learned properly** — Java by default; C/C++, Python or TypeScript if you prefer.
4. **Applied craft** — databases, web development, and four escalating projects.
5. **The job hunt itself** — resume, search strategy, AI as a tool, interviews, negotiation, and the first switch.

Subjects run in that order because the order is the product. You can jump, and
nothing stops you, but the sequence is the thing you are actually paying an
institute for elsewhere.

## The ten principles

> A person with a degree, an internet connection, and no idea where to start
> should be able to reach their first software job **without paying anyone for
> direction**.

1. **Direction is the product, not content.** We curate, sequence and filter — and we say out loud what to skip.
2. **Foundations before tools.** Tools change every 18 months; OS, DBMS, networks, OOP and DSA do not.
3. **Orientation before choice.** Everyone starts at the same place, so the choice that follows is informed rather than random.
4. **One language, learned properly.** We recommend Java and defend it. What we refuse to endorse is learning four languages badly.
5. **This is the IIT curriculum, taught honestly.** The subjects are not secret. We cannot give you the campus; we can give you the syllabus and an honest explanation of each topic.
6. **Job search is a syllabus, not an afterthought.** It converts everything before it into income.
7. **Honest about money and outcomes.** No "100% placement" claims. No guaranteed packages.
8. **The path is data, not code.** Serving a new kind of learner means composing a sequence, not shipping a release.
9. **Free core, forever.** Anything monetised later must be genuinely additional — mentorship, review, cohorts — never the map itself.
10. **Accountability is a feature.** Progress, streaks, resume-where-you-left-off, a player that knows your next lesson. This is what the institute really sold.

## Explicit non-goals

- **Not** a job board. We teach the hunt; we do not broker the roles.
- **Not** a placement agency. No fees, no guarantees, no bonds.
- **Not** an interview-question dump. Patterns and understanding, not 500 MCQs.
- **Not** a maths-heavy CS degree. Discrete maths and calculus are not prerequisites for a software job and we will not pretend otherwise.
- **Not** chasing every trending tool.
