I kept the job and kept the habit. Weekends: study, walk-ins, interviews.

Three months later — **Accenture. 3.6 LPA, ₹24,000 in hand.**

And I got the things the startup could not give me: an orientation, a batch,
friends. I was trained in Salesforce. I worked on three different projects. I
started enjoying my life.

One of those friends was Durgesh, who had come up the same way I did — job
seeker, walk-ins, all of it. When he got his first job I asked him one question,
and his answer is the single most useful thing anybody told me in those years.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-message-circle" color="primary"}
**"Tell me about yourself."**

That is what decides whether you get selected. Not the algorithm question. That
one.
::

Here is my experience of it, stated as precisely as I can:

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Prepared intro" verdict="right"}
  Your odds go up across the whole interview, because the first three minutes set
  what they think they are talking to.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Prepared, but not the intro" verdict="wrong"}
  Fifty-fifty. You can know the material and still lose the room in the first
  question, and you will never be told that is what happened.
  :::
::
