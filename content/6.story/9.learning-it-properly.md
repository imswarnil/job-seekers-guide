So I joined a training institute.

Java. SQL. HTML and CSS. DBMS. OOP. And this time it went in, taught in a way
where I started to actually understand the language rather than memorise the
shape of it.

Two people are responsible for that, and I am naming them because they will never
show up in a search result and they should:

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-graduation-cap" title="Shishir Bhatt sir"}
  Taught me Java in a way that finally made the language make sense, rather than
  making me copy it.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-database" title="S. N. Dixit sir"}
  Taught me SQL. I am not writing SQL every day now, and I still use how he
  taught me to think about a query.
  :::
::

::tech{names="java,sql,html,css,oops" labelled}
::

Here is what I actually did, and it matters more than the institute:

- I never bunked a single class. Not one.
- I made my own notes. Not the handouts. Mine.
- I worked out my own way of answering interview questions, and wrote the answers
  down.
- I wrote **my own example programs** to bring into interviews, so that when
  somebody asked about inheritance I had a thing I had built and could explain,
  rather than the textbook's `Animal → Dog`.

::ai-prompt{title="The thing I would have paid for at this point" model="Any"}
I am learning Java for entry-level interviews. Here is my explanation of
[concept]. Ask me the three follow-up questions an interviewer would ask after
this answer, and tell me which of them I have not actually answered.

  #why
  I did this by hand, badly, alone. It is the loop that took me from
  "I have read about it" to "I can be questioned about it". If you have a tool
  that will do it with you, use it. That is not cheating, that is the thing an
  expensive institute is selling.
::
