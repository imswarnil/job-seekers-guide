## Why this matters in a job

Because work does not arrive as "build a website". It arrives as a ticket with
an ID, a description written by somebody in a hurry, and an estimate you were
asked to give before you understood it. Knowing the pipeline tells you where to
push back and when.

## The pipeline

::steps{level="3"}

### Somebody wants something

A user complains, a salesperson promises, a founder has an idea, a regulator
changes a rule. This is where all software comes from.

### Product turns it into a requirement

Written down, with the thing it is meant to achieve. Good requirements say *why*;
bad ones only say *what*, and you will get plenty of those.

### It becomes a ticket

In Jira, Linear, GitHub Issues — the tool does not matter. A ticket has a title,
a description, an acceptance criterion, and an assignee. When a ticket says
nothing but "fix login", ask for the acceptance criterion. Every time.

### It is estimated and pulled into a sprint

A **sprint** is a fixed window, usually two weeks, holding a set of tickets the
team believes it can finish. The list of everything not yet in a sprint is the
**backlog**.

### You build it

On a branch, as a pull request, reviewed by a teammate.

### It is tested

By automated tests, by QA, or both. On **staging**: an environment that mirrors
production with fake data, so mistakes are cheap.

### It is released

Merged and deployed. In a healthy team this happens many times a week and is
boring. Boring releases are the goal.

### It is reviewed at the end of the sprint

**Demo** — here is what we built. **Retrospective** — here is what made it hard,
and what we change next time.

::

## The words, defined once

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Backlog | Everything wanted but not yet scheduled |
| Sprint | A fixed window of committed work, usually two weeks |
| Story / ticket | One unit of work with an acceptance criterion |
| Story points | A relative size guess. Not hours, however much people treat it as hours. |
| Standup | Daily fifteen minutes: done, doing, blocked |
| Grooming | Meeting where the backlog is clarified and sized |
| Retro | End-of-sprint meeting about process, not people |
| MVP | The smallest version that delivers the value |
| Scope creep | The MVP quietly growing until it is not minimum or viable |

## Estimating, as a junior

You will be asked how long something will take before you know how to do it.
The honest answer, which is also the professional one:

> "I need about half a day to look into it. I will give you an estimate this
> afternoon."

::tip{icon="i-lucide-clock"}
Then, when you do estimate: think of your realistic number and give the range
around it. "Two to four days" is a better answer than "three days", because it
communicates the uncertainty that is genuinely there.
::

## The interview question this answers

**"Do you have experience with Agile?"**

You do not need to have worked a job to answer this well. Describe the loop:
backlog to sprint, standup daily, build and review, demo and retro at the end —
and say what you think it is for, which is delivering something small often
enough to find out you were wrong cheaply.
