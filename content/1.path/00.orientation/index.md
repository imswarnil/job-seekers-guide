Nobody told me any of this before I picked a degree. I chose a branch because it
sounded employable, spent four years being taught around the subject, and only
found out what the job was after I had failed to get one. That is a stupid order
to learn things in, and it is the order almost everybody gets.

So this track goes first, and there is no code in it.

## Who this is for

Two people, and it is worth knowing which one you are.

**Someone who has not started yet**: deciding on a branch, or whether to take a
degree at all. This track exists so that decision is made with information in
front of you rather than with what a relative said at a wedding.

**Someone already committed**: mid-degree, graduated, or changing careers. Read
it anyway. Knowing what the work is, what the roles are and where the money
actually comes from changes what you build and how you talk about it.

## Why a whole week on this

Because the most expensive mistake in this field is not a bad framework choice.
It is four years and a large amount of somebody's money spent on a direction
nobody checked.

::warning
This track will not tell you that software is the right answer for you. It will
give you enough to decide, and one of the possible outcomes is "not this". A
course that cannot say that is selling something.
::

::real-life{title="The conversation this is really for" source="Every family in India with a school-leaver in it"}
A seventeen-year-old, two parents, one uncle with an opinion, and a form due on
Friday. The decision gets made on hearsay about packages, and nobody in the room
has ever seen what the work looks like. Four years later somebody discovers they
dislike it, or that they would have been fine without the degree, or that the
branch never mattered as much as the projects did.
::

::callout{icon="i-lucide-arrow-right"}
You will know what the work is and whether you want it. What you will not have
is any idea how the machine in front of you works, and everything after this
rests on that.
::
