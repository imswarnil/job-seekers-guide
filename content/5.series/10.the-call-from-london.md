## The narration

I asked Durgesh how he got hired. Here is what he told me, and I have never
stopped believing it:

::callout{icon="i-lucide-message-circle" color="primary"}
**"Tell me about yourself."**

That is the question that decides whether you get selected. Not the algorithm
question. That one.
::

My own experience, stated as precisely as I can:

- **Prepared intro** — your odds go up across the entire interview, because the
  first three minutes decide what they think they are talking to.
- **Prepared, but not the intro** — fifty-fifty. You can know the material
  cold and still lose the room in the first question, and nobody will ever tell
  you that is what happened.

It is not a warm-up. It is the frame that everything after it gets read inside.

---

Three years later Durgesh switched jobs and got **21 LPA**.

I was on 5.5.

Same start. Same walk-ins. Same nothing. And the feeling was not envy — it was
*what am I doing?* He had moved and I had stood still, and standing still had felt
like stability right up until that number.

So I learned to interview properly. Here is what happened in about a year:

::timeline{label="What it looks like once you know how"}

  Five times my first salary.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Genpact" date="₹12 LPA" state="done"}
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Dazeworks" date="₹14 LPA" state="done"}
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Cognizant" date="₹15.5 LPA" state="done"}
  The one I took. Past one lakh a month.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="PwC" date="₹21 LPA" state="done"}

  :::
::

From thirteen thousand rupees a month. It looks unreal written down. It looked
unreal while it was happening.

---

Now the part that gets me disagreed with.

I was not happy at Cognizant. Micromanagement, and the way employees were
treated. After **seven months** I decided to leave.

Seven months. Against every piece of advice anybody gives — *stay two years, it
looks bad, recruiters will ask.*

I cracked PwC. Then Twilio.

::note{icon="i-lucide-info"}
I am not telling you to leave your job at seven months. I am telling you that the
two-year rule is a norm, not a law, and that I have been on the other side of it
and it cost me nothing. Decide with information, not with folklore.
::

At Twilio I stayed three-plus years, doing analytics in Salesforce, in
Go-To-Market — and learned what a product company culture actually is, which is a
different thing from what people say it is.

---

Then a call from London.

A company was looking for my skillset. In Budapest. Would I be interested?

I said yes. I cracked it.

I am now in Europe, working as a Salesforce engineer.

---

So here is the only question worth asking about all of that. **Why did any of it
transfer?**

I started with Java and SQL. I do not write much of either now.

::real-life{title="What actually carried over" source="Budapest, today"}
How to think about a query — which S. N. Dixit sir taught me in a classroom in
Bangalore in 2018 — is what I use in Salesforce every week.

The MVC architecture I half-learned at an institute is the architecture of
Salesforce development.

Nothing at the bottom was wasted. It was upgraded.
::

That is what a foundation is for. Not the syntax — the syntax expires. The
foundation is the thing that makes each next thing faster to learn than the last
one. Skip it and every technology is a fresh start, forever.

---

I do not know how you landed here.

This platform is for people like me. People with potential who are lost. Who have
the capability, no guidance, possibly no money, and a family conversation waiting
at home about a safer plan.

I was not talented. I was not passionate about software. I did not clear a single
written round in college.

What I eventually got was a **sequence** — somebody telling me what to learn, in
what order, and how to talk about it. That sequence cost me forty thousand rupees
and a year I did not need to lose.

That is what this is. The sequence, written down and given away.
