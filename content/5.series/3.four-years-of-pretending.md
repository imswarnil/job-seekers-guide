## The narration

Operating systems. DBMS. Java. Data structures and algorithms. Object-oriented
programming. Computer networks.

I am listing them because I want you to notice something. Those are not filler
subjects. That is not a syllabus padded out to fill four years.

**That is the entire job.** Everything I have been paid to do since — every one
of those subjects is load-bearing in it.

And I sat through all of them and understood almost none of it.

---

I want to describe what a lecture actually felt like, because "I didn't
understand" is too clean.

It was not that the words were hard. I could follow a sentence. It was that
nothing ever attached to anything. Paging, semaphores, normalisation,
polymorphism — each one arrived as a definition to be written down, and none of
them ever came back attached to a reason.

Not once in four years did anybody say: *here is a bug you will be handed in your
first job, and this is the subject that fixes it.*

::real-life{title="The sentence nobody said" source="Every lecture, four years"}
"Your service will go down at two in the morning. Requests will pile up. The
health check will time out, and something will kill a process that was working
perfectly and just slowly. The reason will be that a pool of ten was being asked
for by two hundred at once. That is this subject. That is what a semaphore is
for."

Eleven minutes. That is all it would have taken, once, in first year, to make the
next four years mean something.
::

---

So how did I pass?

The same way most of the class passed. You find out which questions come. You
learn the shape of the answer. You write it down convincingly. You get your
seventy percent.

I want to be careful here, because this sounds like an accusation and it isn't.
Nobody was cheating. The system asked a specific question — *can you reproduce
this?* — and I answered it correctly. It simply never asked the other question.

Here is the thing that took me years to understand:

::callout{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle" color="warning"}
I did not fail to learn because I was incapable. I failed to learn because
nothing I was taught was ever connected to anything I could see, and I did not
know that connecting it was allowed, let alone that it was the whole point.
::

---

And this is where the real damage happens, and it is not academic.

By the end of four years I had a completely coherent story about myself: *I am
not a programmer. I don't enjoy this. I'm not that kind of person.*

That story was built entirely out of a teaching method. Not out of me.

It took a training institute in Bangalore, four years later, about six weeks to
demolish it.

## Next time

In my final year I found the thing I actually wanted to do — and I could not say
its name out loud in my own house.
