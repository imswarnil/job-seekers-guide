## The narration

Placement season.

I was not, honestly, all that interested in a job. I was interested in the
channel. But everybody sits the tests, so I sat the tests.

Java. Quantitative aptitude. Written English. Reasoning.

I failed. And I need you to hear what kind of failing this was, because there are
two kinds and people conflate them.

This was not "reached the last round, lost to a better candidate".

I could not clear the **first written round**. The screening one. The one that
exists to reduce a thousand applicants to two hundred before anybody human is
involved.

Not once.

---

I have thought about why, for years, and I can now say it precisely.

That written round is not a test of intelligence and it is not really a test of
knowledge either. It is a test of **preparation for that specific format**. It has
a shape. It has a time budget per question. It has a set of patterns that recur
every year at every company.

Somebody who knows that trains for it. I did not know that. I walked in and tried
to solve the questions honestly, one at a time, like a person, and ran out of
clock.

::compare{caption="Same knowledge. Completely different outcome."}
  :::compare-side{label="What I did" verdict="wrong"}
  Read each question, tried to solve it properly, took as long as it took. Ran
  out of time at question thirty of fifty and left twenty blank.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What clearing it requires" verdict="right"}
  Recognise the pattern in four seconds, know which ten to skip immediately,
  budget the clock. It is a trainable skill and it is almost never taught.
  :::
::

Nobody had ever told me that. Four years in an engineering college and nobody had
ever sat the class down and explained what the first round actually is.

---

And then, one exception. Tech Mahindra.

I got through. And then I got through the next one. And the next.

I reached the **HR round**.

Do you know what that felt like, after months of getting deleted by a machine at
9:40am? It felt like the thing had finally turned. Like the wall had a door in it
after all.

I was rejected.

---

I am not going to dress up what that did.

The word I used about myself, at the time, was loser. Not "disappointed". Not
"unlucky". Not "this is a numbers game and I will keep going". Loser. Somebody
who had been given four years and a degree and had produced nothing.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-heart-crack" color="neutral"}
And the specific cruelty of getting to HR is this: you cannot tell yourself you
were filtered by a machine. A person met you. Several people met you. And then
they said no.
::

---

College ended.

No job. My family had no plan, and neither did I, and a train was going to put me
back in a house where somebody was going to ask me what happens next.

I did not have an answer.
