## The narration

Kota was over. The admission season was on. And the question was: which branch.

I want to reconstruct this conversation as accurately as I can, because I think
it is the most common conversation in Indian engineering and almost nobody writes
it down.

My brother and my guardians told me: **take CSE. Getting a job is easy in CSE and
the market is good.**

That was it. That was the whole analysis. And I said yes, because I had no
counter-argument — I did not have a thing I wanted to do instead.

I took admission at LNCT Bhopal. Computer science.

---

Let me tell you exactly how much I knew about computer science on the day I
enrolled in a four-year degree in it.

I had never written a line of code. Not one. Not in school, not out of curiosity,
not once.

I did not know what a programming language was for. I could use a computer the
way anybody could use a computer.

That is the entire foundation on which the next ten years of my life were built.

---

Now — I am not telling you this as a confession. I am telling you because of what
every syllabus and every course and every YouTube tutorial quietly assumes.

They assume you chose this because you love it.

Watch how a data structures course opens. *"As you know, efficiency is
fascinating…"* Watch how a curriculum is sequenced — it is sequenced for somebody
who is already curious, who will fill the gaps themselves out of interest,
because the writer was that person.

::compare{caption="One of these is the student the syllabus is written for. The other is most of the class."}
  :::compare-side{label="Who the course assumes you are" verdict="neutral"}
  Chose this out of interest. Codes for fun. Will go and find out the bits that
  were skipped, because wanting to know is the default state.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Who I was" verdict="neutral"}
  Chose this because somebody said the job market was good. Will not fill any gap
  on my own, because I do not yet know a gap exists.
  :::
::

There is nothing wrong with either. The failure is that only one of them is
catered for, and the other one — the bigger group — is left to conclude they are
stupid.

---

I had no dreams. I want to say that plainly because the internet is full of
origin stories where someone always knew.

I did not always know. At eighteen I had no idea what I wanted, no passion, no
plan, and a seat in a degree chosen for me on the basis of a job market I had
never seen.

That is not a tragedy. That is just extremely normal, and almost nobody admits it.
