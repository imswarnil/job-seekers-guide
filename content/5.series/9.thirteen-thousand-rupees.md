## The narration

I started giving interviews. Properly this time — with my own notes, my own
answers, my own examples.

And one day I got one.

A startup. Web developer. I was told I was the one selected out of approximately
a thousand people.

---

The package was **1.8 lakh per annum**. Thirteen thousand rupees a month.

I know how that number reads on a screen in a story that ends where this one
ends. So let me tell you what it actually meant, because I was genuinely,
uncomplicatedly happy.

My sister was earning fifteen thousand and sending me eight.

Thirteen thousand meant **she could stop**. It meant I could live on my own money
in a city I had arrived in with nothing. It meant the thing my father had been
worried about, and the thing I had been silently ashamed of since the Tech
Mahindra HR round, was over.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-indian-rupee" color="primary"}
A first salary is not measured against the market. It is measured against what
was happening before it. Mine was measured against a sister sending me more than
half of hers.
::

---

My sister was not happy.

She always thought I deserved better. She thought I was intelligent.

I want to be honest — I think she was right about the first part and I am still
not sure about the second. But that is what having somebody in your corner
actually looks like, and it is not always comfortable. She was not celebrating
thirteen thousand because she had never accepted the frame in which thirteen
thousand was the ceiling.

She was right. It took me three months and one friend to find that out.

---

Because here is the mistake I did **not** make, and it is the one thing from this
episode I would put in front of every first-job person in the country:

**I did not stop.**

I had the job. Weekends were mine. And I kept doing exactly what I had been doing
when I was unemployed — studying, going to walk-ins, giving interviews.

::compare{caption="Both people got the same first job."}
  :::compare-side{label="What most people do" verdict="wrong"}
  Get the first job, exhale, stop studying. The relief is completely
  understandable. Three years later the salary is still roughly the first salary.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What I did" verdict="right"}
  Keep the weekend routine exactly as it was when I was unemployed. The job is
  the floor, not the finish line.
  :::
::

---

Three months later: **Accenture. 3.6 LPA. Twenty-four thousand in hand.**

Double, in twelve weeks.

And I got the things a startup could not give me. An orientation. A batch.
Friends who started the same week I did. I was trained in Salesforce — which,
though I had no idea at the time, is the technology I am still working in from
Europe.

I worked on three different projects. I started, for the first time in this whole
story, enjoying my life.

---

One of those friends was Durgesh. Same route as me — job seeker, walk-ins, no
guidance, all of it.

When he got his first job I asked him one question about how.

His answer is the next episode, and it is the single most useful thing anybody
said to me in those years.
