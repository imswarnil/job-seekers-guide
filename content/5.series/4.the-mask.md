## The narration

Final year. And for the first time since school, I found something I would stay
up for.

I wanted to be a YouTuber. A creator. Something in that direction — I did not
have a clean word for it, and I still think the clean words are mostly marketing.
I wanted to make things and put them in front of people.

I started a channel.

---

Understand what this was, coming from where I came from. Four years of a subject
I did not enjoy, in a degree I did not choose, in a plan nobody had thought
through. And then something that I did entirely on my own initiative, badly, at
night, because I wanted to.

I got to a thousand subscribers.

A thousand people is not a career. But a thousand people is also not nothing, and
at that point in my life it was the only evidence I had that I could do a thing
without being told to.

---

And I told nobody. Not my father. Not my guardians. Nobody at home.

I did it behind a mask.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-venetian-mask" color="neutral"}
I did not have the courage to say, in my family, in my town, that I wanted to
work in a creative field. That is the actual sentence and I am not going to
soften it.
::

I want to be fair to my family here, because this is not a story about villains.
Nobody would have shouted. It is subtler and heavier than that.

In a town like mine, there is a set of things a person can respectably be.
Government teacher. Bank. Engineer with a company name people recognise.
"YouTuber" is not on the list — it is not even understood well enough to be
rejected. Saying it would not have started an argument. It would have started
concern. And concern from people who love you and cannot afford to be wrong about
you is much harder to carry than an argument.

---

So what does the mask cost?

It is not that you cannot do the thing — I was doing the thing. It is that you
cannot get any help with it. You cannot ask advice. You cannot be encouraged. You
cannot be talked out of the bad ideas, either.

You are running the most important project in your life in a locked room, alone,
with no feedback, and you have to hold up the entire belief in it yourself,
every day, with nothing coming back.

That is exhausting in a way that has nothing to do with the work.

::note{icon="i-lucide-info"}
This is not the part of the story where I tell you to announce your dream to your
family. I did not. Episode seven is what I actually did with it, and it is more
boring and much more useful than announcing anything.
::

---

And then placement season started.

I had found the first thing I ever wanted. And the calendar had arrived to ask me
about something else entirely.
