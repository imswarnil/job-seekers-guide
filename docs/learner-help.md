# Help — for learners

Source text for the public help page. Written to be read by someone who is
anxious and short on time.

## Getting started

**Where do I start?**
At the beginning: *What is Software / How the IT Industry Works*. Even if you
have a computer science degree. It is the module that tells you what the roles
actually are, what they pay, and what each team does all day — and it is the
part almost nobody is taught.

**I'm not from a computer science background. Can I do this?**
Yes. A large part of who this was built for is people from mechanical, civil,
B.Sc., commerce, and completely non-technical careers. Your branch decides
where you start, not where you finish.

**Do I need to be good at maths?**
No. Not calculus, not discrete maths, not engineering maths. You can have an
excellent, well-paid career in software without any of it. If you go into
machine-learning research later you will pick up what you need then.

**How long does this take?**
About six months at 12–15 hours a week. Longer if you have less time. The six
months pass either way.

## Money

**Is it really free?**
The core path is free and stays free: orientation, foundations, one language,
projects, and the whole job-search module. No trial, no card.

**What does the subscription get me?**
Members-only courses, and no ads. Nothing else is held back.

**Can I buy one course?**
No — and that is deliberate. Being asked to buy something at every step of a
path is exactly the confusion this platform exists to remove. One decision,
made once, or free.

**Where are my receipts?**
`/account/` → Billing history. Each payment has a printable receipt.

**How do I cancel?**
Through the billing portal linked from any receipt. You keep access until the
end of the period you have already paid for, and every course you finished
stays finished.

## Learning

**I keep forgetting what I learned.**
Normal. Do the quizzes, build the projects, and explain things out loud to
somebody. Reading is not learning; retrieving is.

**Can I skip the foundations and go straight to the tools?**
You can. It is the most common and most expensive mistake people make. The
foundations are what make the tools learnable — and what interviews actually
test.

**Which language should I pick?**
Java, unless you have a reason not to. It has the largest fresher job market in
India by a wide margin. Pick Python for data, TypeScript for frontend, C++ for
systems. One language, properly — the second one takes two weeks once the first
is solid.

**A lesson is locked.**
It is part of a members course. Free preview lessons are marked; everything
else in that course opens with a subscription.

## The job hunt

**How many applications should I send?**
Fewer, better, with referrals, beats hundreds through a portal. The job-search
module has actual numbers.

**I've been rejected a lot.**
The person who built this was rejected 33 times before his first offer, at
₹13,000 a month. Rejection is data: write down which round you failed and what
was asked, fix one thing, go again. That habit is worth more than another
month of studying.

**Should I take a low-paying first job?**
Usually yes. The fresher tag is a door you only need opened once. The two cases
where you should not are in *Offer, Joining & Your First Switch*.
