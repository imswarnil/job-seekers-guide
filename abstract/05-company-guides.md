# Company Guides

## The question people actually ask

Nobody searches for "structured learning path for software engineering". They
search for **"how to get a job in Accenture"**.

That is not a lesser question. It is the same question asked from where the
person is actually standing: they have heard of one company, they know someone
who got in, and they want to know what it takes. A platform that answers only
the abstract version of the question is a platform that never gets found.

## What a company guide is

A page per company answering, in this order:

1. **What kind of place is this** — service, product, GCC, consulting, startup.
2. **What it pays**, as a band, per role, marked fresher or experienced.
3. **How hard it is to get in**, on a five-point scale.
4. **How they hire** — campus, off-campus, referral, portal, consultancy.
5. **The selection process**, round by round, in the order you meet them.
6. **What they test**, with the course that teaches each skill linked.
7. **The guide itself** — the honest editorial part.

## Three rules

### 1. Bands, never individual packages
A named person's salary next to their employer identifies them. Every figure is
a range, per role, and every company carries a **last checked** date shown on
the page. Compensation data goes stale fast, and a confident-looking number
from three years ago is worse than no number — somebody will negotiate against
it and lose.

### 2. Every skill links to a course
A guide that says "you need SQL" and stops leaves the reader exactly where they
started. The entire reason to put this on a learning platform rather than a
blog is that the next step is one click away. Where no course exists yet, the
page says so plainly rather than linking somewhere approximate.

### 3. Honest about difficulty, including upward
Saying Google is hard is easy. The harder honesty is saying that a service
company is *accessible* — because the people this platform is for have often
been told that anything reachable is worthless. Accessible is not a slur. It is
a door, and the guides say which doors are open.

## What these are not

**Not job listings.** No vacancies, no application deadlines, no "apply now".
The structured data is deliberately `Article` + `HowTo` about an
`Organization`, never `JobPosting` — that markup describes a real open vacancy
and search engines surface it as one. Emitting it here would put a fake
vacancy into job results.

**Not endorsements.** A guide to a company is not a recommendation of it. Where
a service agreement is onerous or a package is low, the guide says so.

**Not scraped.** Every guide is written, dated, and revisable. If we cannot
say something honestly, it is left out.

## Editorial checklist

- [ ] Salary as bands, per role, with a last-checked date
- [ ] Selection rounds in the order a candidate meets them
- [ ] What actually decides the outcome, stated plainly
- [ ] The mistake people make at this company
- [ ] Skills linked to courses, or marked as not yet covered
- [ ] Difficulty rated honestly in both directions
- [ ] Nothing stated that cannot be sourced or reasonably observed

## Why this sits in the abstract

Because it is the clearest example of the project's whole thesis: the
information already exists, scattered across forums and hearsay and outdated
blog posts. What is missing is somebody putting it in order, dating it, and
saying which parts matter. That is the product — see
[01-abstract.md](01-abstract.md).
