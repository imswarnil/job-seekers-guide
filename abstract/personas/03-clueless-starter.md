# Persona 3 — The Clueless Starter

**Shorthand:** Rahul, 21, B.Sc. / B.A. / diploma, small town, first in family to
consider IT

## Situation

Wants "a job in computers." Cannot name a single role beyond "software
engineer." Doesn't know the difference between frontend and backend, between a
product company and a service company, between a developer and a tester. Has
watched some YouTube, downloaded three roadmaps, started none of them.

This is the persona the platform is most sharply designed for — because this is
the persona training institutes extract the most money from.

## What they have

- Time, willingness, and a phone or a shared laptop
- No misconceptions to unlearn — a genuine advantage

## What they lack

- The vocabulary to even ask a good question
- Any mental model of what companies do all day
- English/communication confidence, often
- Anyone in their circle who has done this

## What they believe (wrongly)

- "You need to be a genius / very good at maths." — you do not
- "There's one thing called 'coding' and you either can or can't."
- "A paid course is more trustworthy because it's paid."

## Fears

Looking stupid for asking. Being scammed. Choosing wrong and losing a year.

## What the platform does for them

- Starts at **Level 1 — "Start me from zero"**
- **Module 0 is everything for this persona.** Before any code: what software
  is, what an IT company contains, what each team does all day, what roles
  exist, what they pay, what a day looks like. Vocabulary first.
- The *How IT Works* module — git, MVP, sprint, staging, ticket, standup, QA,
  DevOps, product vs service — so they are never lost in a room again
- English/Communication runs as a parallel track, not a gate
- Explicit "you may skip this / you don't need this" guidance to reduce overwhelm
- Small, frequent wins: short lessons, quizzes, visible streak

## Assembled path

`How IT Works (extended) → What Role Suits Me → Foundations (all 9, gentlest
pacing) → Java (from zero) → SQL → Web → Projects 1–4 → English/Communication
(parallel) → Resume → Job Search → Interviews → Offer & Switch`

## Success looks like

Being able to explain, to a friend, what a backend developer does — and then
becoming one.
