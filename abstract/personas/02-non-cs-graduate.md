# Persona 2 — The Non-CS Engineer / Science Graduate

**Shorthand:** Priya, 23, B.E. Mechanical (or B.Sc. Physics / B.Com), Indore

## Situation

Graduated in a branch with no software jobs waiting. Core-industry openings are
few and pay poorly. Everyone around her is "going into IT" and she has decided
to as well — but her degree gave her none of the base.

## What they have

- Discipline and study habits — she cleared a hard degree
- Possibly some MATLAB / Excel / basic C from a first-year course
- Strong motivation, because the alternative is a job she doesn't want

## What they lack

- Every CS foundation. Genuinely from zero.
- Any sense of whether her degree disqualifies her (it does not)
- Confidence in rooms full of CS graduates

## What they believe (wrongly)

- "Companies won't hire a non-CS candidate." — most service and product
  companies hire on skill; the branch matters far less than she thinks
- "I need a second degree / M.Tech / MCA first." — no
- "I'm already three years behind." — she is one focused year behind, at most

## Fears

That the degree is a permanent handicap. That the family investment was wasted.
Explaining the switch to relatives and to interviewers.

## What the platform does for them

- Starts at **Level 1 — "Start me from zero"**
- Foundations taught with **no assumed prior CS**, in full, not skimmed
- Extra emphasis on the "why this subject matters in a job" framing, because
  she has no classroom memory to hang it on
- A dedicated lesson on **telling the branch-switch story** in interviews and
  turning it into an asset rather than an apology
- Projects weighted toward web/product, which demo well and read well on a
  resume

## Assembled path

`How IT Works → Foundations (all 9, full depth) → Java (from zero) → SQL/DBMS →
Web → Projects 1–4 → Branch-Switch Story lesson → Resume → Job Search →
Interviews → Offer & Switch`

## Success looks like

Being asked "you're from Mechanical?" in an interview and having an answer that
makes it a strength.
