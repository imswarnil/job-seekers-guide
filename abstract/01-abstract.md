# Abstract

**Job Seekers Guide** is a guided learning platform for people who want a job in
the software industry and do not know where to start.

Every year, lakhs of students graduate in India — from engineering, science,
commerce, and arts — and discover that a degree is not a job. The gap between
"graduated" and "employable" is filled today by coaching and training
institutes that charge ₹50,000–₹1,50,000 to teach material that is already
available for free on YouTube, on blogs, and in open courseware. They are not
selling knowledge. They are selling **sequence, filtering, and someone telling
you what to do next.**

This platform sells nothing of the sort — it gives that away.

## What it is

A structured, persona-aware learning path builder. A user signs up, tells us who
they are and where they want to reach, and the platform assembles a path:

1. **Orientation** — what the IT industry actually is, before you pick anything.
2. **Foundation** — the computer science subjects that matter in the real world
   (OS, DBMS, Networks, OOP, DSA, Software Engineering, System Design, Web, AI).
3. **One language, learned properly** — Java by default, or C/C++, Python, or
   TypeScript.
4. **Applied craft** — databases, web development, and 4 escalating projects.
5. **The job hunt itself** — resume, search strategy, using AI as a tool,
   HR and technical interviews, negotiation, and the first switch.

Content is delivered as courses and lessons inside a self-hosted WordPress LMS
(`job-seekers-lms`). Paths are composable: an admin can assemble any sequence of
existing courses, articles, videos, and quizzes into a named path, so new
personas can be served without writing new content.

## What makes it different

| Training institute | Job Seekers Guide |
|---|---|
| ₹80,000 upfront, non-refundable | Free core path |
| One syllabus for everyone | Path assembled from your answers |
| Teaches tools, skips foundations | Foundations first, tools second |
| Placement "assistance" as a sales line | Job search taught as a skill with its own syllabus |
| Ends at course completion | Continues through offer, joining, and first switch |

## Who it is for

Four primary personas — the fresh CS graduate with no offer, the non-CS
graduate, the completely clueless starter, and the career changer from a
non-tech field. See [personas/](personas/).

## The thesis

Getting a job in software is not a talent problem. It is a **navigation
problem**. Content is abundant and free; direction is scarce and expensive. We
invert that.
