# Vision and Principles

## Vision

> A person with a degree, an internet connection, and no idea where to start
> should be able to reach their first software job **without paying anyone for
> direction**.

## Principles

### 1. Direction is the product, not content
The lectures exist. The docs exist. The blog series exist. We curate, sequence,
and filter — and we say out loud what to skip. A lesson that says *"skip this,
here's why"* is as valuable as one that teaches.

### 2. Foundations before tools
Tools change every 18 months; OS, DBMS, networks, OOP, and DSA do not. We teach
the layer that survives, then the tool on top of it. A learner who understands
what a process is will learn any framework; the reverse is not true.

### 3. Orientation before choice
No one picks "backend engineer" meaningfully on day one. Everyone starts with
the same module — *What is Software / How the IT Industry Works* — so the choice
that follows is informed rather than random.

### 4. One language, learned properly
We recommend **Java** and defend the recommendation. Learners may pick C/C++,
Python, or TypeScript instead. What we refuse to endorse is learning four
languages badly.

### 5. This is the IIT curriculum, taught honestly
The subjects are not secret. They are the same ones taught at the best
institutes in the country. The difference there is teaching quality and peer
group — not the syllabus. We can't give you the campus; we can give you the
syllabus and an honest explanation of each topic.

### 6. Job search is a syllabus, not an afterthought
Resume, search strategy, using AI properly, HR rounds, technical rounds,
negotiation, offer evaluation, first switch. Taught with the same seriousness as
DBMS, because it converts the other seven modules into income.

### 7. Honest about money and outcomes
No "100% placement" claims. No guaranteed packages. We say plainly: the first
offer may be small, take it anyway, here's why, here's what happens next.

### 8. Paths are data, not code
Every path is assembled in the admin console from existing courses, articles,
videos, and quizzes. Serving a new persona means composing a new path — not
shipping a release.

### 9. Free core, forever
The core path — orientation, foundations, one language, projects, job search —
stays free. Anything monetised later must be genuinely additional (mentorship,
review, cohorts), never the map itself.

### 10. Accountability is a feature
Progress bars, streaks, resume-where-you-left-off, a dashboard that knows your
next lesson, a wall of people who made it. This is what the institute really
sold. We build it in.

## Explicit non-goals

- **Not** a job board. We teach the hunt; we don't broker the roles.
- **Not** a placement agency. No fees, no guarantees, no bonds.
- **Not** an interview-question dump. Patterns and understanding, not 500 MCQs.
- **Not** a maths-heavy CS degree. Discrete maths and calculus are not
  prerequisites for a software job and we will not pretend otherwise.
- **Not** chasing every trending tool. Foundations, one language, real projects.

## How success is measured

| Signal | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Onboarding completion | Did the flow actually produce a path? |
| Module-1 completion | Did orientation land before choice? |
| Path completion rate | Is the sequence realistic? |
| Projects submitted | Proof-of-work created, not just watched |
| Job-search module completion | The module that converts learning to income |
| Success stories submitted | The only metric that finally matters |
