# Module 5 — English & Communication

**Course code:** `SOFT-101` · **Runs in parallel with everything, from week 1.**

## Why this is a subject and not an afterthought

People are rejected for this constantly, and almost never told that's why. The
feedback comes back as "not a culture fit" or "communication gap" or nothing at
all.

It is not about accent. It is not about vocabulary size. It is about being able
to **explain what you built, ask a clear question, and disagree politely** — in
a language that is nobody's first language in most Indian offices, including
your interviewer's.

## Framing, stated on lesson 1

> Your English does not need to be beautiful. It needs to be *clear*. The person
> interviewing you is very likely also speaking their second language. Nobody is
> judging your grammar. They are judging whether they can understand your
> thinking — and whether you'll be able to explain a bug to a colleague at 6pm
> on a Friday.

## Lessons — one per week, alongside the technical path

### Speaking about your work
1. Explaining what you built — the three-sentence structure
2. Explaining a technical thing to a non-technical person
3. Thinking out loud — the skill technical interviews actually measure
4. "Tell me about yourself" — a two-minute answer, built and rehearsed
5. Saying "I don't know" well — the answer that gains more credibility than a
   bluff

### Writing at work
6. Email that gets a reply — subject lines, structure, brevity
7. Asking a good question — what you tried, what happened, what you expected
8. Writing a ticket, a bug report, a status update
9. Slack/Teams etiquette — brevity, threads, tone, when to just call

### Interview-specific
10. Small talk before the interview starts
11. Answering behavioural questions — the STAR structure, without sounding
    robotic
12. Asking *them* questions — and why having none is a bad signal
13. Salary conversations without flinching
14. Following up after an interview

### Working with people
15. Disagreeing politely with a senior
16. Taking code-review feedback without taking it personally
17. Giving a status update in a standup — 30 seconds, three parts
18. Saying no, and asking for more time

## Practice format

- **Record yourself.** Every speaking lesson ends with a recorded 60-second
  answer, kept private, listened back to. The discomfort is the point and it
  fades by week three.
- **Write for real.** Writing lessons produce a real artifact — an actual email,
  an actual bug report.
- **Weekly, not crammed.** 30 minutes a week for the length of the path beats a
  two-day "soft skills workshop", which is what institutes sell and which works
  on nobody.

## Never a gate

This module never blocks a technical module. A learner who is anxious about
English must still be able to progress in code — and by the time they've built
four projects, they'll have something worth explaining, which is when this
module starts landing.
