# Module 7 — Resume, Job Search & Using AI

**Course codes:** `JOB-101` (resume), `JOB-102` (search), `JOB-103` (AI)
**Duration:** 3 weeks

> This is the module that converts everything above it into income. It is taught
> with the same seriousness as DBMS, because **job search is a skill** — I
> learned it the hard way over 33 rejections, and it is the reason I moved from
> ₹1.8 LPA to Accenture in three months.

## `JOB-101` — Your Resume · 1 week

### How a resume is actually read
1. The 7-second scan — what a recruiter's eye does, in order
2. **ATS** — what applicant tracking systems are, how they parse, and why
   beautiful two-column Canva templates get silently discarded
3. Why "references available on request", photos, DOB, and marital status hurt
   you

### Building it
4. The one-page structure that works for freshers
5. **Writing a bullet** — verb + what you did + technology + result. Before/after
   examples, ten of them
6. The projects section — where the value is, using blocks from Module 6
7. The skills section — honest levels, and never listing what you can't defend
8. Education, without a CGPA if it doesn't help you
9. Handling gaps, low marks, a non-CS branch, or a career change — directly, in
   a line, without apology
10. Formatting — one column, standard fonts, PDF, filename `Name_Role.pdf`

### Finishing
11. Tailoring per application — the 10-minute version that actually moves the
    response rate
12. Your LinkedIn — headline, about, projects, and being findable
13. Your GitHub as a portfolio — pinned repos, READMEs, a green graph
14. **Resume review checklist** — 25 items, self-audit before you send anything

## `JOB-102` — Finding the Job · 1 week

### Where the jobs are
1. The channels, ranked by actual conversion: **referrals > LinkedIn direct >
   company careers pages > Naukri/Indeed > consultancies > mass-apply portals**
2. Why the portal everyone uses converts worst — and when to use it anyway
3. Consultancies and staffing firms — how they work, what's legitimate, and the
   fee scams to walk away from
4. Off-campus drives, hiring challenges, hackathons

### Referrals — the highest-leverage skill in the module
5. Why a referred application is read and a portal application often isn't
6. Finding the right person — alumni, ex-colleagues, second-degree contacts
7. **The cold message that gets a reply** — templates, with the reasons each
   line exists
8. Following up without becoming a nuisance

### Running it like a process
9. **The application tracker** — using your own Project 3
10. Volume, honestly — what an actual weekly target looks like
11. Timing — when postings go up, when to apply, why day 1 beats day 10
12. **The rejection log** — for each rejection: which round, what was asked,
    what you'd change. This one habit is what turned my 33 rejections from
    despair into a system.
13. Handling silence, ghosting, and "we'll get back to you"
14. When to widen — location, role, company size, salary expectation

### Staying alive
15. Rejection is data, not a verdict — the mental model, stated plainly
16. Routine, sleep, and not letting the search consume the day
17. Who to talk to when it's been four months

## `JOB-103` — Using AI to Get a Job · 1 week

> AI is the single biggest advantage a fresher in 2026 has that a fresher in
> 2017 did not. Used well it is a research assistant, a mock interviewer, and an
> editor. Used badly it produces a generic resume and an interview you can't
> survive.

### Use it for
1. **Resume feedback** — the prompts that get real critique instead of praise
2. **Tailoring** — matching your resume to a job description, honestly
3. **Company research** — what they do, their stack, their recent news, before
   you walk in
4. **Mock interviews** — the prompt to make an AI interview you properly and
   grade you hard
5. **Explaining concepts** — the tutor that never gets bored of your fifth
   "but why"
6. **Debugging** — how to ask so you learn instead of just pasting a fix
7. **Cold messages and emails** — drafted by AI, edited by you, in your voice
8. **Practice problems** — generated at your level, with explanations

### Do not use it for
9. Writing your resume for you — recruiters recognise it, and you can't defend
   claims you didn't write
10. Doing take-home assignments — you will fail the follow-up discussion
11. Live-interview cheating — it ends careers and is increasingly detected
12. Learning instead of building — a chat log is not a skill

### The honest part
13. **What AI means for entry-level jobs** — what's actually changing, without
    doom or hype
14. **Being the fresher who uses AI well** — an interview advantage, if you can
    talk about it thoughtfully

## Outcome

An ATS-safe one-page resume, a working LinkedIn and GitHub, a tracker with live
applications in it, a referral habit, a rejection log, and an AI workflow that
makes each week's applications better than the last.
