# Module 8 — Interviews: HR & Technical

**Course codes:** `INT-101` (HR/behavioural), `INT-102` (technical)
**Duration:** 2 weeks

## The rounds, mapped first

Lesson 1 of this module is a map, because most candidates don't know what's
coming:

```
Application
   ↓
Screening call (HR)              ← people fail here and never know why
   ↓
Online test / DSA round          ← service companies: aptitude + coding
   ↓
Technical round 1                ← fundamentals, your projects
   ↓
Technical round 2                ← depth, system design (or a second panel)
   ↓
Managerial round                 ← fit, ownership, how you handle pressure
   ↓
HR round                         ← a real round, not a formality
   ↓
Offer
```

For each round: who's in it, what they're deciding, how long it runs, and what
"failing" it looks like.

## `INT-102` — Technical · 1 week

### The coding round
15. What DSA rounds actually test — and how to talk while you code
16. Thinking out loud — the behaviour that decides borderline candidates
17. When you're stuck — the three moves that keep you in the interview
18. Handling a wrong answer without collapsing

### The fundamentals round
19. **The 50 questions that come up everywhere**, mapped to their foundation
    subject — OS, DBMS, networks, OOP
20. "What happens when you type google.com and press enter" — the answer, at
    three levels of depth
21. OOP questions that go past definitions
22. SQL at a whiteboard

### The project round
23. **Presenting your project in three minutes** — problem, approach, stack,
    hardest part, what you'd do differently
24. The follow-up questions that expose people who didn't build it
25. "Why did you choose this technology?" — having a real answer
26. Talking about what broke, and what you learned from it

### System design (for level 3/4 and experienced candidates)
27. How to approach an open design question
28. The structure: requirements → estimate → components → data → scale →
    trade-offs
29. Two worked examples, spoken aloud

### Practice
30. **Mock technical interview** — with AI (using the `JOB-103` prompts), with a
    peer, and against a self-review rubric
31. **The post-mortem habit** — after every real interview: which round, what
    was asked, what you froze on, what you fix this week

## The post-mortem, again

It appears in Module 7 and Module 8 because it's the highest-value habit in
both:

> After every interview, before the feeling fades: write down every question you
> were asked, mark the ones you fumbled, and pick one to fix this week.
>
> Do that for ten interviews and the eleventh feels different. That's not
> motivation — it's just what happens when rejection becomes a feedback loop
> instead of a verdict.

## Outcome

A candidate who knows what each round is for, has rehearsed the answers that
recur, can defend every line of their own resume, and treats each interview as
an input to the next one.
