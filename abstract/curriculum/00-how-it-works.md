# Module 0 — What is Software / How the IT Industry Works

**Course code:** `CS-000` · **Duration:** 2 weeks · **Mandatory for everyone,
cannot be deselected, always first.**

## Why this module exists

Because you cannot choose a destination you cannot see.

There are N number of tools, N number of roles, N number of skills, and every
roadmap on the internet contradicts the last one. A beginner facing that picks
randomly, or freezes. Both waste a year.

The foundation is what actually decides your career — but knowing what *exists*
is what lets you choose which foundation to lean on. So: orientation first,
choice second, code third.

This module is also the one self-taught developers most often skip, and it shows
in interviews — they can write a function but cannot explain what a sprint is,
what QA does, or why the product manager is asking them for an estimate.

## Lessons

### Part 1 — What is software, really
1. **What is software** — instructions, hardware, and everything in between
2. **How a program actually runs** — you write text, the machine runs
   electricity; what happens between
3. **What "IT industry" means** — the shape of a ₹250 billion sector in plain
   language
4. **Product vs Service companies** — TCS/Infosys/Accenture vs Google/Zoho vs
   startups; different work, different hiring, different pay curves
5. **Where the money comes from** — how a company that makes software makes
   money, and why that determines what your job feels like

### Part 2 — Inside an IT company
6. **The teams** — Engineering, QA, DevOps, Product, Design, Data, Support,
   Sales, HR, Finance — what each one actually does all day
7. **A day in the life** — standup, tickets, code review, deployment, a
   production incident, walked through hour by hour
8. **How work arrives at your desk** — requirement → ticket → sprint → build →
   test → release
9. **Who you'll report to and work with** — team lead, manager, architect,
   scrum master, PM: what each actually does

### Part 3 — The vocabulary (the "How IT Works" glossary)
10. **Version control & Git** — what it is, why every job assumes it, the six
    commands you'll use daily
11. **MVP, scope, backlog, roadmap** — how products get decided
12. **Agile, Scrum, sprint, standup, retro** — the ritual language of every
    modern team
13. **Dev, staging, production** — the three worlds, and why you never test in
    the third
14. **Bug, ticket, hotfix, rollback, release** — the incident vocabulary
15. **API, frontend, backend, database, server, cloud** — the architecture
    vocabulary
16. **CI/CD, code review, pull request, merge conflict** — the workflow
    vocabulary
17. **On-call, SLA, uptime, incident, postmortem** — the operations vocabulary

### Part 4 — Roles and reality
18. **The roles, honestly** — for each of ~12 roles: what they do on a Tuesday,
    what skills it needs, what it pays for a fresher in India, what it pays at
    5 years, how hard it is to enter, and how easy it is to leave
19. **Skills vs tools vs foundations** — why the foundation outlives the tool,
    with 10-year examples
20. **How people actually get hired** — campus, referrals, portals, LinkedIn,
    consultancies, and which ones work for whom
21. **Money, honestly** — fresher ranges by role, city, and company type; what
    grows a salary and what doesn't
22. **What "experience" means** — why the fresher tag is a one-time door, and
    why the first offer is a door and not a destination

### Part 5 — Choose (or don't, yet)
23. **Role Reality Check** — an honest self-assessment: do you want to build
    things, break things, ship things, analyse things, or talk to people about
    things?
24. **How to choose without regret** — why the choice costs weeks to change and
    not years, and permission to be wrong

## Variants by level

| Level | Variant |
|---|---|
| 1 — from zero | All 24 lessons, full pace, extra glossary support |
| 2 — know a little | All 24, faster |
| 3 — can code, can't get hired | Parts 2–4 only (14 lessons) — the industry and money parts they're missing |
| 4 — already working | Parts 4–5 only (7 lessons) — roles, money, and honest positioning |

## Outcome

By the end, a learner can:

- explain what an IT company contains and who does what
- follow a conversation between engineers without being lost
- name the role they're aiming for, and say why
- state what a fresher in that role earns, and what it becomes in five years
- explain the difference between a foundation, a skill, and a tool

## Assessment

A 20-question quiz on the vocabulary — the only quiz on the platform that is
purely about words, because words are the barrier here.
