# Module 9 — Offer, Joining & Your First Switch

**Course code:** `JOB-201` · **Duration:** 1 week

The module most platforms don't have, because most platforms consider their job
done at "you got an offer." Mine wasn't. Everything that changed my situation
happened *after* the first offer.

## Part 1 — You have an offer

1. **Reading an offer letter** — CTC vs in-hand, the components, the variable
   part, the joining bonus with a clawback, the retention clause
2. **CTC ≠ salary** — a worked example, ₹4.5 LPA on paper turned into a monthly
   number
3. **Bonds and service agreements** — what's normal, what's exploitative, what's
   unenforceable, and what to check before signing
4. **Negotiating as a fresher** — what's actually movable (usually little, but
   not nothing), and how to ask without risking the offer
5. **Multiple offers** — how to compare beyond the number: what you'll learn,
   the tech stack, the team, the city, the exit options
6. Declining an offer without burning the relationship

## Part 2 — Take the small offer

> ### Read this if the number is disappointing.
>
> My first offer was **₹13,000 a month. ₹1.8 LPA.** After 33 rejections. It was
> less than friends were making, less than my family expected, and less than the
> training institute had implied.
>
> I took it. Deliberately.
>
> Because the **fresher tag is a door you only need opened once.** The moment
> you have a company name, a joining date, and real work on your resume, every
> conversation afterwards changes. You stop being someone asking to be believed
> in, and start being someone with a track record. Recruiters return calls.
> Referrals become possible. The screening round stops being an interrogation.
>
> Three months after that ₹13,000 job, I was at Accenture on a Salesforce track,
> earning a multiple of it — and the only reason that was possible is that I had
> taken the small offer instead of waiting for a good one.

7. **The first offer is a door, not a destination**
8. When to accept a low offer, and the two cases where you genuinely shouldn't
9. What "the fresher tag" is worth, arithmetically
10. Planning the exit on day one — without becoming the person who's always
    leaving

14. Probation — what's actually being assessed

## Part 4 — The first switch

18. **When to switch** — the signals, and the norms worth ignoring
19. **What changes for experienced candidates** — the resume, the rounds, what's
    probed, what's forgiven
20. **Knowing your market rate** — how to research it, and how to stop

22. **Negotiating with leverage** — the conversation that's genuinely different
    the second time
23. Counter-offers — why accepting one usually ends badly

## Part 5 — The loop closes

26. **Give back** — write your success story for the Wall of Success. Somebody
    at rejection number 12 needs to read it.
27. **Mentor one person.** Answer questions for the people behind you. This is
    how the free thing stays free.

## Outcome

A learner who can read an offer, take the right one for the right reasons,
survive probation, plan the next move deliberately, and negotiate it from
strength — and who knows that the process they just learned is repeatable for
the rest of their career.
