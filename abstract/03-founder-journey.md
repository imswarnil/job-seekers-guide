# The Journey

The founding story, kept here because every design decision in this platform
traces back to some part of it.

## 2017 — Graduated, not placed

Campus placements came and went. I was not one of the names on the list. The
degree was in hand and the plan was not.

The immediate feeling isn't panic — it's **fog**. You don't know what you don't
know. You don't know which subject matters, which language to pick, whether you
should be doing DSA or building a website, whether the problem is your skills or
your resume or your college's tier.

> **Design consequence:** the platform's very first module is not a technical
> subject. It is *"What is Software / What is the IT industry?"* — because you
> cannot choose a destination you cannot see. See
> [curriculum/00-how-it-works.md](curriculum/00-how-it-works.md).

## The training institute

I did what most people in that fog do: I paid an institute.

It wasn't useless — it gave me a schedule and people around me in the same
position. But sitting there I realised the lectures were things I could have
watched for free. What I was actually paying for was **structure and company**.

> **Design consequence:** free core path, plus everything the institute actually
> provided — an ordered path, progress tracking, a dashboard that says "you're on
> lesson 12 of 40", streaks, and a wall of people who made it.

## 33 rejections

Thirty-three. I counted, because counting was the only thing that made it feel
like a process instead of a verdict.

Somewhere around the twentieth I stopped treating rejection as a judgement and
started treating it as **data**. Which round did I fail? What did they ask?
What did I say when I froze? What would I say next time?

That shift — from "I'm not good enough" to "round 2 keeps killing me, fix round
2" — is the single most valuable thing I learned that year.

> **Design consequence:** the job-search module teaches rejection as a
> debuggable process — an application tracker, a rejection log, per-round
> post-mortems. See [curriculum/07-job-search.md](curriculum/07-job-search.md).

## First job — ₹13,000/month (₹1.8 LPA)

I took it. Knowingly, deliberately, at a salary that barely covered living.

Because the **fresher tag** is a one-time door. Once you have a company name, a
joining date, and real work on your resume, every conversation afterwards is
different — you are no longer someone asking to be believed in, you are someone
with a track record.

> **Design consequence:** we tell people this honestly. The platform does not
> promise ₹12 LPA at entry. It tells you the first offer is a *door*, not a
> destination — and then teaches you what to do the day after you walk through
> it.

## Three months later — Accenture, Salesforce track

I did not stop learning the day I got the offer. That is the part that gets
skipped in every version of this story that gets told.

I kept studying, and I studied the hunt itself: how a resume is actually read,
where to apply and when, how referrals work, how to answer "tell me about
yourself" so it lands, how to talk about salary without flinching. Three months
in, I switched — into Accenture, onto a Salesforce stream, at a number that
changed the arithmetic of my life.

> **Design consequence:** the path does not end at "you got a job." It ends at
> *"you know how to get the next one."* The final module covers the first
> switch, the notice period, and how to keep compounding.

## What I actually learned

1. **The subjects that matter are few, and they are knowable.**
2. **One language, learned deeply, beats five learned shallowly.**
3. **Projects are the proof; certificates are not.**
4. **Job search is a skill, separate from engineering skill.**
5. **The first offer is a door. Take the door.**
6. **Rejection is data, not a verdict.**
7. **Nobody is coming to hand you a plan. So here is one.**

## Why build this now

Because the same fog is still being sold at the same price, to people with less
money and more competition than I had — and now the free content is even better
and even more abundant than it was in 2017. The only thing still missing is the
map.
